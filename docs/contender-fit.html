Version 1, built from NHL team statistics for 15 past seasons. It is an estimate from past patterns, not a prediction of who will win the Cup, and not odds or a betting line. The next versions will add shot quality, zone time and goaltending in context.

Does it work?

The honest test: for each past season the score is built from the other seasons, then used to rank that season's teams, and we look at where the four real conference finalists landed. The benchmark is simply ranking by points %.

Short answer
Slightly, but not provably. Best version: Model 3: + power kill (PP% + PK%). Averaged over five checkpoints its AUC is 0.778 against 0.760 for points % (a gain of +0.017; the noise margin is about +/-0.053).
Season by season
It ranked the real final four better than points % did in 45 season-checkpoints and worse in 28 (out of 75; the rest tied).
When it helps
Early: at Nov 1 the gain is +0.045 (+/-0.076). By the end of the season it is +0.003 (+/-0.029): a plausible reason is that a long record already reflects what Corsi and special teams add.
What each idea adds
+ Corsi: +0.015; + power kill (PP% + PK%): +0.006; + special-teams volume and net goals: -0.008 (change in average AUC from the previous version; compare with the noise margin of about +/-0.053).
How predictable is it?
At the end of the regular season the real final-four teams average rank 6.9 by points % (a random list would average about 16); at Nov 1 it is 11.1. Many final-four teams are simply not obvious from the standings: that is the nature of the playoffs, not a flaw in the stats.
Single stats on their own
Ranking by Corsi alone gives AUC 0.690 against 0.760 for points %: clearly worse, so controlling shot attempts does not by itself make a contender. Goal difference alone gives 0.740: no better than points % for picking the final four.
Not tested yet
Slot / high-danger shot share, zone time, breakouts, goaltending in context, depth (top-15 center, elite D pair). Those need MoneyPuck and play-by-play data and are the next versions.

Is "top 5 in most of the factors" a rule for finding the champion?

A popular idea: champions are usually near the top of the league in several key areas, so teams that match should be favorites. The four areas here are record (points %), goal difference, Corsi (shot-attempt share at 5-on-5) and the power kill (power-play % plus penalty-kill %). A random team wins the Cup about 3% of the time (1 in 31).

Such teams do win more often than a random team, but most champions do not meet the bar, and most teams that do meet it do not win. It narrows the field; it does not find the winner.

What this does not know yet

  • How dangerous a team's shots are (slot and high-danger shots), and how much of the goalie's results come from the defense in front of him.
  • How much of the game a team spends in the offensive zone, and how cleanly it breaks out.
  • Depth: a top-line center, an elite defense pair, or a goalie who gets hot in the playoffs.
