What we found

Base rate
Of the teams in a spot at Thanksgiving, 24% missed; at Jan 1, 20%; at the trade deadline, 10%.
Flags that matter
Most consistent (flagged teams missed more than equally safe teams at all three checkpoints, clearly so at one or more): Weak shot share (Corsi), Record flatters goals. Hints (small samples): Running hot (PDO), Weak special teams. No clear effect: Penalty trouble, Cold lately. With 18 flag-and-checkpoint tests, a couple of results this strong can happen by chance, so read them as 'worth a look', not proof.
Stacking
Thanksgiving: 9 of 15 teams with 3+ flags missed (60%) against 49 of 225 with 0-2 flags (22%); Jan 1: 8 of 16 teams with 3+ flags missed (50%) against 41 of 224 with 0-2 flags (18%); Trade deadline: 3 of 8 teams with 3+ flags missed (38%) against 21 of 232 with 0-2 flags (9%). The 3-or-more group is small (8-16 teams) and is one of four groups, so treat the gap as a hint.
Honest test
Adding the number of flags to the cushion changes the hold-out AUC by +0.012 on average (margin about +/-0.029): within the noise, so the flags do not clearly improve on simply asking how close to the line the team was.
How to use it
Treat flags as reasons to look closer, not predictions. They are most useful for explaining WHY a team's spot may be less safe than the standings suggest. Early in a season (about 20 games) the flags are noisy.

What the flags mean

Running hot (PDO)
Shooting % + save % is in the top quarter of the league: luck that tends to even out.
Weak shot share (Corsi)
Takes a smaller share of the shot attempts than most teams (bottom quarter).
Record flatters goals
Ranks at least 25 percentile points higher on points than on goal difference: wins came in close games.
Weak special teams
Power-play % + penalty-kill % in the bottom quarter of the league.
Penalty trouble
Short-handed more often than almost anyone: top quarter in times short-handed per game.
Cold lately
Points % over the last 10 games in the bottom quarter of the league.

About this page

The question: Being in a playoff spot is not the same as being safe. Do warning signs make a team that is "in" more likely to miss?

Six warning signs, checked on teams that were in a playoff spot at Thanksgiving, Jan 1 or the trade deadline in 15 past seasons (2009-10 on). A flag is a reason to look closer, not a prediction. Terms such as cushion, PDO and AUC are explained on the About page.

How it was tested, and the limits

  • The six flags and their cut-offs (top or bottom quarter of the league that season) were fixed before looking at results.
  • Each flagged team is compared with teams the same distance above the playoff line, so a flag cannot win just by meaning "barely in".
  • With 6 flags at 3 checkpoints, a couple of strong-looking results can happen by chance, and the groups with 3 or more flags are small.
  • Only team statistics are used: no injuries, trades, schedule or shot quality.
