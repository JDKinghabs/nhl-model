What we found

Short answer
Creating chances is what goes with winning. Chance share predicts the results of games it has not seen at +0.50, against +0.46 for shot-attempt share (Corsi) and +0.46 for expected goals. It still adds something after shot share is taken out (+0.26, noise margin 0.10), and after expected goals as well +0.15 (margin 0.10).
Time in the zone, or what you do with it?
Both count, and they are different skills (they move together at only +0.10). With the other taken out, offensive-zone time predicts unseen games at +0.45 and chances per minute in the zone at +0.20 (noise margins about 0.05).
The zone-time estimate
Four ways of estimating zone time from the play-by-play were compared with the NHL's tracking. The best on 2021-22 to 2023-24 ('half to each event's zone', gaps up to 30 seconds) agreed at +0.86, and at +0.77 on 2024-25 and 2025-26, which it had not been chosen on. The website's team tables use the NHL's real numbers; the tests use the estimate because they need game-by-game numbers.
Does it help pick contenders?
Added to the contender score and tried on seasons the score had not seen, chance share changes the AUC by -0.002 (noise margin 0.015): within the noise.
Champions
Cup winners averaged the 78th percentile on chance share (100 = best), finalists the 80th, teams that missed the playoffs the 34th.

Time in the zone, or what you do with it?

A team's chances per 60 minutes are its share of time in the offensive zone times the chances it creates per minute there. The two parts move together only a little (+0.10), so a team can get its chances either way. Each part is tested here with the other taken out.

How it is measured

  • Scoring chance: a shot on goal, a missed shot or a goal at 5-on-5 from the shaded area: from the goal posts out to the faceoff dots, then straight out to the top of the circles. Blocked shots are left out because the NHL records where the block happened, not where the shot was taken.
  • Zone time: the share of 5-on-5 time spent in the offensive, neutral and own zone. In the team tables it is the NHL's EDGE puck tracking.
  • Chances per 10 minutes in the zone: chances created divided by minutes spent in the offensive zone, times 10. The same against, in the own zone.
  • Chance share: the team's chances divided by all chances in its games.

The tests need zone time game by game and back to 2009-10, which the NHL's tracking does not give. So they use an estimate from the play-by-play: the time between two events is credited to the zone the play was in. Four versions were compared with the NHL's tracking on 2021-22 to 2023-24; the best ("half to each event's zone", gaps up to 30 seconds) agreed at +0.86, and at +0.77 on 2024-25 and 2025-26, which it had not been chosen on.

About this page

The question: How much time does a team spend in the offensive zone, how many scoring chances does it create from that time, and which of those goes with winning?

All numbers are at 5-on-5 in the regular season. Scoring chances come from the NHL's play-by-play shot coordinates. In the team tables, zone time is the NHL's own EDGE puck tracking (2021-22 on). The tests cover the finished seasons from 2009-10 to 2025-26 (without the shortened 2019-20 and 2020-21), and use an estimate of zone time from the play-by-play because they need game-by-game numbers. Terms are explained on the About page. 5-on-5 ice time, Corsi and expected goals are from MoneyPuck.com, used here for non-commercial purposes with credit.

How it was tested, and the limits

  • The chance area is a fixed choice made before the tests, not tuned to the results. It uses shot location only: rebounds, rush chances and passes before the shot are not counted separately.
  • The same-season numbers are flattered, because goals are chances too. The unseen-games test is the fair one.
  • Zone time in the tests is an estimate. It tracks the NHL's own numbers well but not perfectly, which makes the zone-time results a little weaker than they really are.
  • A link is not proof of cause, and these are season-level results: they say how strong a team is, not who wins a single game.
