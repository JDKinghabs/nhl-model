What we found

What drives goals against
Across teams, defense (the quality and quantity of shots allowed) accounts for about 55% of the differences in goals against per game, goaltending about 52% (the shares add to more than 100% because the two are slightly positively related, correlation +0.10: teams that allow more danger get a little more help from their goalies).
What repeats
Defense repeats from before Jan 1 to after (correlation 0.65 for expected goals against); goaltending results repeat much less (0.18 for goals saved above expected, 0.31 for raw save %). So the defensive part of goals against is the stable part, and most of what looks like goalie skill over a half season is noise.
Bailed out vs let down (after Jan 1)
Teams labeled 'goalie bailing out the defense' earned -1.6 standings points over the rest of the season relative to what their record predicted (margin +/-1.4); teams 'let down by the goalie' +1.8 (+/-1.4); teams good at both +0.5. A sign of regression to the mean, if the first is negative and the second positive.
Do the numbers improve the forecast?
Gain in forecasting points % after Jan 1 beyond record and goal difference (R-squared): Defense - expected goals against per 60 (5v5): +0.034; Defense - high-danger shots against per 60 (5v5): +0.011; Goalie - goals saved above expected per 60: +0.007; Goalie - raw save % (all situations): +0.003. Same hold-out test and noise level as the popular-stats check.
The hot playoff goalie
No regular-season signal clearly foresees a hot playoff goalie: every link is 0.13 or lower (goaltending results, the last 20 games, raw save %, the starter's previous season, defense quality), and about 0.13 is the noise level with about 240 playoff runs. Hot playoff goaltending looks mostly unforeseeable from the regular season. One more way to look: 17 of the 45 teams whose regular-season goaltending ranked top 3 among the playoff teams that played 7+ games also had top-3 goaltending in the playoffs (38%, against 29% by chance: a lift of 1.3x), so being good helps a little, but most good regular-season goalies do not get hot and most hot ones were not the best in the regular season. Champions' playoff goaltending averaged the 72nd percentile among playoff teams (and 93% of champions had positive playoff GSAx), and playoff goaltending is linked to rounds won at 0.27: a hot goalie matters in the playoffs, it just is not visible in advance.
Not covered yet
The free files have no playoff data for individual goalies, and the team numbers blend starter and backup. Shot-level data (also on MoneyPuck's page) would let us do the goalie-by-goalie playoff version.

About this page

The question: Is a goalie bailing out a weak defense, or is a strong defense being let down by its goalie? And can anything foresee a hot playoff goalie?

Goals against split into two parts with MoneyPuck's shot-quality data: defense (how many goals the shots allowed should produce: expected goals against per 60 minutes at 5-on-5) and goaltending (goals saved above expected, "GSAx"). 15 past seasons, 2009-10 on. Terms are explained on the About page. Shot-quality data on this page is from MoneyPuck.com, used here for non-commercial purposes with credit.

How it was tested, and the limits

  • The team numbers blend every goalie a team used, including the backup. Playoff data for individual goalies is not in the free files.
  • "Expected goals" is one reasonable shot-quality model, not the truth: shot quality it cannot see (screens, passes before the shot) shows up as goaltending.
  • "Defense" here means shot quality allowed, not individual defenders.
  • Forecast tests are built on the other seasons and used on the one they have never seen, with the same noise margins as the other pages.
